# Founder lenses and surface adaptation

## One lens per post

- Industry thesis: a convention in the category the founder believes is wrong or incomplete.
- Architecture decision: why it was built one way instead of an easier alternative.
- Operator lesson: what the change teaches about reliability, support, security or accountability.
- Founder reality: the prioritisation, constraint, rejected shortcut or manual work behind the outcome.
- Implementation pattern: what buyers or builders should consider when adopting the capability.
- Honest trade-off: speed versus control, breadth versus permissions, cost versus convenience.

## Narrative questions

1. What triggered the work?
2. Which shortcut or market default was considered?
3. Why was it not enough?
4. What was built instead?
5. What changed in the founder's understanding?

Every post carries one tension, one visible proof and one honest difficulty.

## Hard rules

- No number that is not in the commits, the release, a knowledge note or the founder's answer.
- No claim that users asked for or loved something unless someone reported it in writing.
- No fabricated anecdotes, customer conversations or internal debates; use a real decision, constraint or test instead.
- First person for the founder's decisions, the company name for product behaviour.
- The company-page version and the founder version differ in hook, order and closing; at most one shared sentence.

## Surface shapes (defaults; the team's voice note wins)

| Surface | Shape |
|---|---|
| Professional network, founder profile | First person, about 80 to 220 words, strong first line, one idea, at most two hashtags, a link only after the insight if the policy allows |
| Short-post network | One idea, plain builder tone, within the character limit, links only if the policy allows |
| Newsletter or long form | One thesis, specific headline, one call to action, canonical link to your site |
| Community forum | The problem first, what changed, what was hard, a real question, disclosure, no link unless the community rules allow it |

## When the week has nothing to show

Say so plainly. Do not inflate a chore into a feature. Either skip, or post the problem in the founder's own sentence ("Spent the week replacing a library that kept breaking builds. Nothing new to see yet, but deploys stopped failing.").
