# Comparison page structure

## Switching trigger gates (all must pass)

1. Recurring: at least three independent complaints in the last 12 months. One angry review is an anecdote.
2. Current: confirmed on the competitor's live pricing page or docs today.
3. Factual: rewrite emotion as fact. "Their support is bad" becomes "Support on the starter plan is email only (pricing page, checked <date>)".
4. Sourced: keep the URL and check date for every claim.

Trigger categories: pricing (seat minimums, forced annual billing, overage fees), lock-in (no export, long cancellation windows), limits (rate limits, caps), support (ticket only on lower tiers), product (missing integration, abandoned feature).

## Section order for a head-to-head page

1. H1: "[You] vs [X]: [the one-line difference]".
2. Two-sentence verdict in the first 50 words. Sentence one says when X wins, sentence two when you win. Leading with the rival's strength makes the rest believable, and retrieval weights the opening most.
3. Trigger table, 4 to 8 rows: Trigger | X | You | Source.
4. Pricing math at three sizes in the product's real unit (seats, runs, contacts), monthly cost on the plan each size is forced onto, URL and check date under the table. Round nothing in anyone's favour.
5. "Switch from X": numbered steps with the real export and import path and honest timing.
6. Where X is the better choice: at least three bullets. Mandatory.
7. FAQ: 3 to 5 questions phrased the way buyers ask them in reviews and threads.
8. One CTA matching the switch, not a generic "get started".

## Alternatives page differences

- Open with the top two or three switching triggers in plain words, then the list.
- 4 to 7 real alternatives; place yourself where you honestly rank for the trigger the page targets.
- Each entry is a self-contained block: verdict line, best for, trade-off, starting price. Self-contained blocks stop answer engines from pinning another tool's limit on you.

## Persona lists ("best [category] for [persona]")

- Ship only where you are an honest top-three pick for that persona. If you fit but are not top three, ship only for a named sub-case where you win.
- H1 matches the persona phrase exactly; the first sentence names the top pick and why for this persona.
- 4 to 7 tools in identical blocks with one persona-specific detail each, plus a "how we picked" section with 3 to 5 criteria.
- 4 to 6 persona lists per batch at most.

## Hard rules

- Zero unsourced competitor claims and no claim older than the day it was checked.
- Never say a competitor lacks a feature without checking their docs that day.
- No "both are great" verdicts.
- Title at most 60 characters, description 140 to 160, both containing "[You] vs [X]" or "[X] alternative".
- Wire discovery: the comparison index page, two sibling comparisons and the pricing page link to each new page.
