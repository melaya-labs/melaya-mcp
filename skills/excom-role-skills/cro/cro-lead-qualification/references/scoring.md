# Lead scoring table

## Default qualified-lead definition

A lead is qualified when first-party evidence shows at least one of: a verified account, a first successful use of the product, a checkout start, or a written request for a plan, a seat, an integration or a call. Propose this to the owner if the knowledge base has no definition.

## Firmographics (0 to 25)

| Points | Signal |
|---|---|
| 20 to 25 | In a priority audience from the positioning note, team size and budget plausible |
| 10 to 19 | Adjacent audience, or priority audience with unclear size |
| 0 to 9 | Outside the audiences, student, or unknown company |

## Intent (0 to 25), first-party signals first

| Points | Signal |
|---|---|
| 25 | Checkout started, or a written request for a plan or seats |
| 20 | First successful use of the product (run, answer, task) |
| 12 | Verified and came back on another day |
| 8 | Booked a call or asked a specific product question |
| 5 | Signup only |
| 0 | No signal, or the only signal is the lead's own claim |

## Timing (0 to 25)

| Points | Signal |
|---|---|
| 20 to 25 | A dated need: launch, hiring post this week, tool renewal, deadline named in writing |
| 10 to 19 | A need without a date |
| 0 to 9 | Exploring, no need stated |

## Solution match (0 to 25)

| Points | Signal |
|---|---|
| 20 to 25 | One product clearly does the job they described |
| 10 to 19 | A product fits with setup or a workaround |
| 0 to 9 | Needs something the products note says is not available |

## Rules

- One product per lead, named exactly as in the products note.
- State weights and thresholds in the output so the owner can challenge them.
- Keep partner attribution in the note for partner-referred leads.
- Copy no personal data beyond name, company and the business signal.
