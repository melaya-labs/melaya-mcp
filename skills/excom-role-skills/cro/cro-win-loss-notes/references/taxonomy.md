# Win-loss taxonomy and counting rules

## Default tags

| Tag | Use when the buyer's reason is about |
|---|---|
| Product fit | The product does or does not do the job they described |
| Missing capability | A named feature or integration they needed |
| Setup or activation | Connecting accounts, models, devices, installs, first success |
| Price or plan shape | Cost, the plan structure, seats, limits, billing terms |
| Trust and security | Data handling, compliance evidence, vendor risk |
| Support | Response speed or quality |
| Timing | Budget cycle, priorities, team change |
| Relationship | Contact left, champion lost, partner preference |
| Competitor | A named alternative chosen (use the buyer's name for it) |

Keep the buyer's specific mechanism inside the tag: three "familiarity" losses may be three different things (paperwork already on file, team already trained, compliance already trusting the incumbent).

## Source priority

1. The buyer's own words (call transcript, reply, interview).
2. Interviewer paraphrase or call summary, labelled.
3. CRM and billing records for outcome, product, segment, dates.
4. Team-entered reasons, as evidence of belief only.
5. Market changes, for context, never as cause on their own.

## Counting rules

- Show "appears in N of M outcomes with buyer evidence".
- Attach the exceptions to every pattern; a pattern without exceptions is a story.
- State sample size and bias (who answered, which channel).
- Segment only when each bucket has at least three outcomes with buyer evidence.
- A market change explains outcomes only if it happened before they decided and the buyer mentions it.
- End with hypotheses worth testing, each with a test and an owner.
