# Objection drivers

Every objection has a surface statement and a driver. Answer the driver.

## "It's too expensive"

| Driver | Signal | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| Real budget limit | Budget cut, small team, student | Point to the free start or the smallest paid option in the pricing note. Never offer a discount |
| Value not seen yet | Never reached a first success | Route to the activation path first; price talk later |
| Plan shape | Needs one surface or feature, sees a full plan | Name the smaller product or plan that fits, from the pricing note |
| Comparison | "X is cheaper" | Ask what they compare (scope, limits, who pays inference), then answer with facts |
| Anchoring or haggling | No pressure signal | Hold the published price calmly; the owner decides any exception |

## "You don't have X"

| Driver | Approach |
|---|---|
| Unfamiliarity | Check the products note and public docs first; it may exist under another name |
| Genuine blocker | Say plainly it is not available. Never promise a date. Log it for the CPO |
| Comparison with a competitor | Ask how they would use it; answer with the closest real capability |
| Nice to have | Acknowledge, show how users solve it today |

## "We already use Y"

Ask what job Y does for them, then name the one product that does that job better or alongside Y, from the competition and positioning notes.

## "My team won't adopt it"

Route to the CXO activation path for that product. Adoption problems are setup or change problems, not pricing problems.

## Security and compliance

State only what the knowledge base documents. Read the claims policy note for claims never to make. If the note is missing, do not claim any certification, audit, penetration test, SLA, RPO, RTO or service credit, and ask the owner.

## Reply rules

- Under 120 words, plain text, no markdown.
- One product per reply.
- Missing features are stated as missing, never as coming.
- No second reply the same day unless the sender answered.
