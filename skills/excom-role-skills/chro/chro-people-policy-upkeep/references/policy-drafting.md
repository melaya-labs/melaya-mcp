# Policy drafting, fairness and privacy checks

## Skeleton
Purpose, scope (who and which situations, including contractors), definitions, policy statements (numbered 1.1, 1.2), roles and responsibilities, procedure (or link), exceptions, breaches, related documents, version control and review date.

## Clause rules
- One obligation per clause.
- "must" for requirements, "may" for discretion, "should" for expectations.
- Name the actor, the trigger, the timeframe and the evidence: "Your manager must respond within <X> working days."
- Define a term once and use it identically everywhere.
- Words like "will", "entitled", "guarantee" can read as contractual: flag for the CLO.
- Policy may be more generous than a legal minimum, never less. Mark every legal dependency "Verify".

## Discretion
For every judgement call: who decides, against which criteria, how fast, how it is recorded, how to ask for a review. Unstructured discretion is where inconsistency and discrimination risk live.

## Fairness check
Test each clause against part-time and contract staff, remote and cross-time-zone workers, people with disabilities or caring duties, people on leave, people with low digital access. Remove proxies for protected characteristics. Add an adjustment route where a rule could disadvantage someone.

## Privacy check
If the policy collects data (monitoring, device checks, evidence, health information): what, why, who sees it, retention aligned to the retention schedule.

## Revision table
Clause | current text | proposed text | reason | evidence | reviewer
Keep existing numbering where possible. Add a plain-language change summary for people affected.

## Readability
Grade 8 to 10, short sentences, active voice, "you" for the reader, headings that answer questions.
