# Question bank, scorecard and decision record

## Question patterns
- Behavioural: "Tell me about a time you <did the competency>. What was the situation, what did you do, why, and what happened?"
- Situational: "A <partner/customer/colleague> asks for <something outside policy>. What do you do?" Use the team's real rule as the expected answer.
- Probes: "What was your part, not the team's?", "What would you do differently?", "How did you know it worked?"
- Work sample: a task from the real job, timeboxed, paid if it takes more than an hour (owner decides), same brief for every candidate.

## Scoring rules (print on every scorecard)
1. Take notes during the interview; write what was said, not impressions.
2. Score each answer against the anchors straight after, alone, before any discussion.
3. No score without a note.
4. "Insufficient evidence" is a valid score; do not fill gaps with assumptions.
5. What does not count: confidence, polish, nerves, accent, appearance, talking time, shared background.

## Interviewer briefing (one page)
Purpose of the stage, the competencies and questions in order, timings, what does not count as evidence, how to answer candidate questions about the company honestly (stage, team size, how AI executives and approvals work), adjustments offered.

## Debrief agenda
1. Each scorer reads scores and evidence per criterion, before any overall view.
2. Discuss differences of 2 or more points on evidence only.
3. Name any criterion below the agreed minimum.
4. Recommendation per candidate with reasons in job terms.

## Decision record template
Role | date | stages run | interviewers
Candidate id | criterion scores | key evidence | recommendation | reasons
Decision (owner) | date | signature line
