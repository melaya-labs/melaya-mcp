# Anchors, evidence and question fairness

## Writing anchors (levels 1, 3, 5; 2 and 4 sit between)
- Each anchor lists two to four things the evidence contains, not adjectives.
- A strong anchor has a specific example, the person's own actions with reasons, a result they can point to, and holds up under probes.
- A weak anchor has general statements, "we" without the person's part, or no result.
- Replace evaluative words ("good", "excellent", "impressive", "poor") with content.
- Pitch the strong anchor at the level of the role, not above it.
- Allow evidence from paid work, volunteering, open-source work, study or caring where the criterion permits.

## What does not count as evidence
Confidence or polish of writing style, photo, name, age, nationality, address, accent, school prestige, gaps in employment, shared interests or background, knowing someone in the company, length of the application.

## Question fairness check
For every screening question or form field:
1. Name the criterion it tests. A scored question that tests nothing is removed.
2. Remove questions about age, date of birth, family, pregnancy, health, disability, religion, origin, nationality (beyond right to work), sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status, union membership.
3. A real requirement (shift pattern, travel, time zone overlap, lifting) is asked as: state the requirement, then "can you meet this, with adjustments if needed?"
4. One question, one thing; plain words; the same for every candidate.
5. Offer adjustments in every invitation.

## Redaction before scoring
Remove or tokenise: names, emails, phone numbers, addresses, photos, social handles, dates of birth, graduation years. Keep a private key map in the source system only, never in the draft.
